Top panel — the 20% effect:

The y-axis is how tightly bound the remnant is (more energy radiated as gravitational waves = more bound).

Look at where the lines end up on the right side: the green line (ρ\rho

Bottom panel — what detectors would actually see:

This is the gravitational-wave spectrum — what frequency the signal comes out at and how loud it is.

The inset zooms in: the peaks shift by ~100 Hz between models.

Why this matters for CE and ET in plain language:

Cosmic Explorer = proposed US detector with 40 km arms (10× LIGO), plus a 20 km detector tuned specifically for this 2000–4000 Hz band where those peaks sit.
Einstein Telescope = proposed European underground detector, also ~10× more sensitive than current detectors.
Both are designed to finally see this post-merger signal that current detectors cannot detect (LIGO couldn't see it even from GW170817 at only 40 Mpc away).
The 20% louder signal from flavor mixing means ~33% more space volume where CE/ET can catch these signals, and the ~100 Hz peak shift is right at the edge of what these detectors can measure — meaning they could potentially tell us whether neutrino flavor conversion is happening inside the remnant just from the gravitational-wave signal alone.
